Lecture 1 — Enamel Structure & Histology I

Properties · composition · the enamel rod

🦷 Definition & Origin
  • •The hardest calcified tissue in the body; it forms the protective covering of the crown.
  • •Acellular (no cells) · non-vital · insensitive · cannot be replaced or regenerated once lost.
  • •Origin: epithelially derived (ectodermal). The forming cells (ameloblasts) are lost at eruption, so enamel cannot renew itself.
المينا أصلب نسيج في الجسم وهي عديمة الخلايا ولا تتجدد، لأن الخلايا المكوِّنة لها (ameloblasts) تُفقد عند بزوغ السن.
📐 Physical Properties
  • •Color: opaque enamel → grayish white · translucent enamel → yellowish white; it depends on the degree of calcification.
  • •Thickness: maximum 2–2.5 mm at the cusps of molars and premolars → thins to a knife edge at the cervical margin.
  • •Hardness: due to the high mineral content and the crystallite arrangement; permanent enamel is harder than deciduous; fluoride increases hardness.
  • •Brittleness: increases with age, undermining (loss of dentin support) and in a non-vital tooth.
  • •Permeability: enamel acts as a semi-permeable membrane; mainly from saliva to the outer layer, and less from the pulp (through dentin) to the inner layer.
يعتمد لون المينا على درجة التكلس، وهي أسمك ما تكون عند حدبات الأضراس، وتصبح هشة عندما تفقد دعم العاج من تحتها.
⚗️ Chemical Composition & Methods of Study
  • •96% inorganic (hydroxyapatite crystals) · 4% organic + water (enamel proteins: amelogenins & enamelin) forming a fine network between the crystals.
  • •Ground section: enamel is retained → studied under the light microscope (longitudinal and cross sections).
  • •Decalcified section: minerals dissolve and organic material is washed away → enamel is extremely difficult to study.
تبلغ نسبة المعادن في المينا ٩٦٪، ولذلك تُدرس بالقطاع المطحون (ground section) فقط.
🧱 The Enamel Rod (Prism)
  • •The structural unit of enamel; studied by number · direction · diameter · course.
  • •Number: ≈ 5 million (lower central incisor) → ≈ 12 million (upper first molar).
  • •Direction: perpendicular to the DEJ; vertical at the top of the crown (cusp); oblique toward the occlusal surface. In the cervical area: permanent rods slope oblique root-wise, deciduous rods run more horizontally.
  • •Diameter: 3–4 µm near the ADJ → up to 8 µm near the surface (ratio 1 : 2).
  • •Course: wavy from the ADJ, straight just before the surface; twisted at the cusp tip and incisal edge = gnarled enamel. The first-formed and last-formed enamel are non-prismatic.
  • •Rod sheath: the boundary between prisms; more organic and slightly less mineralized than the prism.
العمود (prism) هو وحدة بناء المينا: يزداد قطره ناحية السطح، ومساره متموج ويلتوي عند قمة الحدبة فيسمى gnarled enamel.
👁️ Hunter–Schreger Bands
  • •An optical phenomenon: alternating dark and light bands produced by changes in rod direction.
  • •Best seen in longitudinal ground sections with reflected light; found in the inner two-thirds of enamel; absent in gnarled enamel.
  • •Possible causes: change in rod direction · variation in calcification · alternate zones with different permeability and organic content.
حزم هنتر-شريجر ظاهرة بصرية فقط، تنتج عن تغير اتجاه العمدات.
🔑 Sub-microscopic Structure
  • •Cross section (T.S.): key-hole or fish-scale pattern (oval, rounded or hexagonal). Longitudinal section (L.S.): cylindrical rods separated by less-calcified dark lines = cross striations.
  • •Key-hole: prism (head) → highly calcified · prism sheath → incomplete envelope, less calcified · interprismatic region (tail) → highly calcified like the prism.
  • •Crystals: parallel to the long axis of the prism centrally, then flare laterally toward the prism boundary (a function of Tomes’ process of the ameloblast).
  • •Prism sheath is less calcified because its crystals are not tightly packed, leaving more space for organic material.
في نمط ثقب المفتاح: الغمد (sheath) هو الأقل تكلساً، أما الـ prism والـ interprismatic region فشديدا التكلس.

✍️ أسئلة مقالية متوقعة بإجابات مختصرة ووافية. اضغط «إخفاء الإجابات» لتجيب بنفسك أولاً ثم اكشف الإجابة.

❓ 1. Define enamel and mention its main properties.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •The hardest calcified tissue in the body; it forms the protective covering of the crown.
  • •Acellular, non-vital, insensitive, and cannot be replaced or regenerated once lost.
❓ 2. Why can enamel not regenerate?
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel is epithelially derived (ectodermal).
  • •The ameloblasts that form it are lost as the tooth erupts, so enamel cannot renew itself.
❓ 3. Mention the physical properties of enamel.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Color: opaque (grayish white) or translucent (yellowish white) according to the degree of calcification.
  • •Thickness: 2–2.5 mm at the cusps → knife edge at the cervical margin.
  • •Hardness: high mineral content + crystallite arrangement; permanent enamel is harder than deciduous; increased by fluoride.
  • •Brittleness: increases with age, undermining and a non-vital tooth.
  • •Permeability: semi-permeable membrane, mainly from saliva to the outer layer.
❓ 4. Write on the chemical composition of enamel.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •96% inorganic: crystalline calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite).
  • •4% organic + water: enamel proteins (amelogenins and enamelin) forming a fine network between the crystals.
❓ 5. Compare ground and decalcified sections in the study of enamel.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Ground section: enamel is retained and can be studied under the light microscope (longitudinal and cross sections).
  • •Decalcified section: minerals dissolve and organic material is washed away, so enamel is extremely difficult to study.
❓ 6. Describe the enamel rod (prism).
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •The structural unit of enamel.
  • •Number: 5 million (lower central incisor) to 12 million (upper first molar).
  • •Direction: perpendicular to the DEJ, oblique toward the occlusal surface; cervical area: root-wise in permanent teeth, more horizontal in deciduous teeth.
  • •Diameter: 3–4 µm near the ADJ, up to 8 µm near the surface (1 : 2).
  • •Course: wavy from the ADJ, straight near the surface, twisted at the cusp tip and incisal edge.
❓ 7. What is gnarled enamel?
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •The twisted course of the enamel rods at the cusp tip and incisal edge.
  • •It gives the enamel extra strength, and Hunter–Schreger bands are absent in this region.
❓ 8. Write on Hunter–Schreger bands.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Optical phenomenon: alternating dark and light bands caused by changes in rod direction.
  • •Best seen in longitudinal ground sections with reflected light; found in the inner two-thirds of enamel; absent in gnarled enamel.
  • •Causes: change in rod direction · variation in calcification · alternate zones of different permeability and organic content.
❓ 9. Describe the key-hole pattern of the enamel rod.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Seen in cross section (also fish-scale: oval, rounded or hexagonal).
  • •Prism (head): highly calcified · prism sheath: incomplete envelope around the prism, less calcified · interprismatic region (tail): highly calcified like the prism.
❓ 10. Why is the prism sheath less calcified than the prism?
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Its crystals are not tightly packed, leaving more space for organic material.
  • •So it has more organic content and looser crystal packing.
❓ 11. Compare deciduous and permanent enamel.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Hardness: permanent enamel is harder than deciduous enamel.
  • •Rod direction (cervical area): oblique root-wise in permanent teeth, more horizontal in deciduous teeth.

Lecture 2 — Enamel Structure & Histology II

Incremental lines · hypocalcified structures · ADJ · surface structures

📅 Incremental Lines of Enamel
  • •Cross striations (short increment): the daily rhythm of the ameloblast; about 3–4 µm apart = one day of enamel deposition.
  • •Brown striae of Retzius: weekly rhythm = the weekly rest period of ameloblasts. In a ground longitudinal section they arise from the ADJ, run upward and outward around the cusp tip and do not reach it; in a ground transverse section they look like concentric circles (tree rings). Broadening = prolonged rest periods in metabolic disturbances.
  • •Perikymata: the external manifestation of the striae of Retzius; shallow furrows on the surface, parallel to each other and to the CEJ; 30 per mm at the cervical area → 10 per mm near the occlusal/incisal edge.
  • •Neonatal line: an accentuated brown stria, found in deciduous teeth and first permanent molars; caused by the sudden change in nutrition and environment at birth (prenatal enamel / postnatal enamel).
الخطوط المستعرضة (cross striations) يومية، وخطوط ريتزيوس (striae of Retzius) أسبوعية، والـ perikymata هي مظهرها الخارجي على سطح المينا، والخط الوليدي علامة الولادة.
🪨 Hypocalcified Structures — Lamellae
  • •Enamel lamellae: fissure-like defects extending for varying depth from the enamel surface.
  • •Type A (developmental): forms during enamel formation, before calcification · Type B: after enamel formation, before eruption · Type C: after eruption.
  • •Classification (as given in the slide): developmental (true) = Type A only · non-developmental (false) / cracks = Type B, Type C and the accidental crack.
الـ lamellae شقوق في المينا: النوع A يتكون أثناء التكوين، والنوع B قبل البزوغ، والنوع C بعد البزوغ.
🌿 Tufts & 🧵 Spindles
  • •Enamel tufts: less mineralized areas projecting from the ADJ for 1/5–1/3 of the enamel thickness; look like tufts of grass; developmental (abrupt change in direction of groups of rods from the scalloped ADJ); branched, with a greater concentration of enamel proteins; best seen in transverse sections.
  • •Enamel spindles: hypocalcified, mostly at the cusp tip and incisal edge; caused by odontoblastic processes entrapped in enamel; in ground sections the processes disintegrate and are replaced by air (dark in transmitted light); short, straight, thin; best seen in longitudinal ground sections.
الـ tufts تمتد من الـ ADJ وهي أقل تمعدناً، أما الـ spindles فتنتج عن محاصرة زوائد الخلايا البانية للعاج داخل المينا.
🔗 The Amelodentinal Junction (ADJ)
  • •A scalloped line with the convexities directed toward the dentin.
  • •The scallops create undercuts once thought to cause the firm attachment.
  • •Now known: the firm attachment is due to interdigitation between the fibrils of the first-formed dentin and the organic matrix of the first-formed enamel, and between the hydroxyapatite crystals deposited during mineralization of both.
التصاق المينا بالعاج لا يعود إلى التعرجات وحدها، بل إلى تشابك الألياف والبلورات بين أول عاج وأول مينا يتكونان.
🧊 Surface Structures
  • •Outer structureless (prismless) enamel: about 30 µm thick; present in all deciduous teeth and 70% of permanent teeth (commonly cervical); crystals parallel to each other and perpendicular to the striae of Retzius; highly mineralized and resists caries.
  • •Perikymata: 30 per mm at the CEJ → 10 per mm near the occlusal/incisal edge.
  • •Rod ends: concave depressions, shallow at the cervical region and deeper near the occlusal/incisal surface.
  • •Cracks: listed as the fourth surface structure (no further details in the lecture).
الطبقة السطحية عديمة التركيب (structureless enamel) سُمكها ٣٠ ميكرون، شديدة التمعدن وتقاوم التسوس.
⏳ Age Changes of Enamel
  • •Attrition · color changes · modification of the surface layer (ionic exchange) · decreased permeability.
تغيرات المينا مع العمر: تآكل، وتغير في اللون، وتعديل الطبقة السطحية بالتبادل الأيوني، ونقص في النفاذية.

👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Cross striations: daily rhythm of the ameloblast, 3–4 µm apart.
  • •Brown striae of Retzius: weekly rhythm (rest period of ameloblasts).
  • •Perikymata: surface expression of the striae of Retzius.
  • •Neonatal line: an accentuated brown stria at birth.
❓ 2. Write on the striae of Retzius.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Weekly rhythm = the weekly rest period of ameloblasts; brown bands in ground sections.
  • •Ground longitudinal section: arise from the ADJ and run upward and outward around the cusp tip without reaching it. Ground transverse section: concentric circles like tree rings.
  • •Broadening = prolonged rest periods in metabolic disturbances.
  • •At the cervical area they reach the surface and form perikymata.
❓ 3. Write on perikymata.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •The external manifestation of the striae of Retzius: shallow furrows where the striae end on the surface.
  • •Continuous around the tooth, parallel to each other and to the CEJ.
  • •30 per mm at the cervical area (CEJ) → 10 per mm near the occlusal/incisal edge.
❓ 4. Write on the neonatal line.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •A more apparent brown band (an accentuated stria of Retzius).
  • •Found in deciduous teeth and first permanent molars (teeth partly formed before and after birth).
  • •Cause: disturbance of enamel formation at birth due to the sudden change in nutrition and environment.
❓ 5. Mention the types of enamel lamellae.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Type A (developmental): mild stimulus, during enamel formation before calcification; contains enamel matrix; restricted to enamel; unerupted tooth.
  • •Type B: severe stimulus, after formation and before eruption; contains cells of the enamel organ; reaches the ADJ.
  • •Type C: severe stimulus, after eruption; contains organic material from saliva; may reach dentin; the most common.
  • •Developmental (true) lamellae = type A only; types B and C (and the accidental crack) are non-developmental (false).
❓ 6. Write on enamel tufts.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Less mineralized areas projecting from the ADJ for 1/5–1/3 of the enamel thickness; look like tufts of grass.
  • •Developmental: abrupt change in direction of groups of rods from the scalloped ADJ.
  • •Branched, with a greater concentration of enamel proteins; best seen in transverse sections.
❓ 7. Write on enamel spindles.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Hypocalcified structures seen mostly at the cusp tip and incisal edge.
  • •Cause: odontoblastic processes entrapped in enamel during its formation.
  • •In ground sections the processes disintegrate and are replaced by air (dark in transmitted light); short, straight, thin; best seen in longitudinal ground sections.

📘 Definition & Physical Properties
  • •Mineralized, living, vital tissue; capable of repair by new layers deposited by the odontoblasts; forms the main bulk of the tooth; covered by enamel (crown) and cementum (root).
  • •Color: light yellowish, darker with age. Hardness: less than enamel, harder than cementum and bone; harder in permanent than deciduous.
  • •Resiliency: viscoelastic, prevents fracture of the overlying brittle enamel. Thickness: 3–10 mm or more.
  • •Radiograph: more radiolucent than enamel, more radiopaque than cementum and bone. Permeability: due to the tubules; decreases with age.
العاج نسيج حي يصلح نفسه بواسطة الخلايا البانية للعاج (odontoblasts)، ويتميز عن المينا بأنه مرن (viscoelastic).
🧪 Chemical Composition & Study
  • •70% inorganic (hydroxyapatite) · 20% organic (collagen fibers 90%, lipids, proteoglycans, non-collagenous proteins) · 10% water.
  • •Enamel: 96% inorganic; dentin: 70%. Decalcified section shows the organic part; ground section shows the inorganic part.
🕳️ Dentinal Tubules
  • •The unit structure of dentin; traverse its whole thickness; closely packed toward the pulp (smaller pulpal surface).
  • •They contain the odontoblastic processes; the cell bodies lie along the inner aspect of dentin (pulp border).
  • •Primary curvature: S-shaped (first curve convex rootward); straight in the root and under the cusp/incisal edge. Secondary curvature: the spiral track of the odontoblasts.
  • •Diameter: 2.5 µm near the pulp → 1.2 µm (mid root portion) → 900 nm near the ADJ (ratio 4 : 1 as stated in the lecture).
  • •Lateral branches: right angle, unite with adjacent processes. Terminal branches: near the ADJ, divide into 2 (may explain increased sensitivity). Profuse branching makes dentin permeable → pathway for caries.
الأنيبيبات العاجية هي وحدة بناء العاج: أوسع عند اللب وأضيق عند الـ ADJ، وتحتوي زوائد الـ odontoblasts.
🧬 Odontoblastic Process & Peri-odontoblastic Space
  • •Diameter: 3–4 µm near the pulp, tapering to 1 µm further into dentin.
  • •Near its base (predentin): mitochondria, ER, ribosomes, microtubules and filaments in a linear pattern. In calcified dentin: microtubules, microfilaments and vesicles for transport and discharge.
  • •Peri-odontoblastic space: between the process and the tubule wall; tissue fluid and dentinal lymph pass through it.
🧱 Inter-tubular vs Intra-tubular Dentin
  • •Inter-tubular: between the tubules; the main bulk; interwoven network of type I collagen with apatite crystals.
  • •Intra-tubular (peri-tubular): a collar lining the tubules; hyper-mineralized with little collagen; appears as translucent rings in transverse ground sections.
🔮 Inter-globular Dentin & 🌱 Tomes’ Granular Layer
  • •Inter-globular dentin: hypo-mineralized areas where globular zones of mineralization failed to fuse; a defect of mineralization (not matrix); tubules pass through it uninterrupted; seen in circumpulpal dentin just below the mantle dentin; increases with vitamin D deficiency and excess fluoride.
  • •Tomes’ granular layer: a constant feature of root dentin next to the cementum; black granules in ground sections; smaller, does not follow incremental lines, tubules do not cross it; due to looped terminal portions of the tubules and/or interference with mineralization.
الـ inter-globular dentin يوجد في التاج (مناطق تمعدن كروية لم تلتحم)، أما طبقة Tomes’ granular layer ففي الجذر بجوار الملاط.

📏 Incremental Lines & Junctions
  • •Von Ebner lines: normal rhythmic daily deposition, 4–8 micron in the crown (much less in the root). Contour lines of Owen: more accentuated von Ebner lines (matrix mineralization disturbance); longitudinal ground section. Neonatal line: discussed in enamel.

🧬 Classification According to Development
  • •Primary: formed during development until root completion (mantle + circumpulpal); rapid; structural.
  • •Secondary: after root completion, throughout life, on the entire pulp surface; very slow unless stimulated; protective response.
  • •Tertiary: in response to pathologic stimuli, in localized areas; reactive or reparative.
⏳ Age Changes — Primary Dentin
  • •Sclerotic (transparent) dentin: tubules occluded — the processes undergo fatty degeneration then calcification (calcium most likely from dentinal lymph); due to normal aging or mild stimuli. Effects: ↓ conductivity, ↓ permeability, slows caries, prolongs pulp vitality, ↑ brittleness.
  • •Dead tracts: severe stimulation destroys or damages the processes; the empty tubules fill with air and look black in transmitted light; bounded by a narrow zone of sclerotic dentin.
العاج المتصلب هو عاج انسدت أنيبيباته بالتكلس، أما الـ dead tracts فأنيبيبات فارغة مملوءة بالهواء بعد مؤثر شديد.
🧱 Regular Secondary Dentin
  • •Continuing deposition after root completion along the entire pulp surface at a much slower rate; increased by a mild stimulus; deposition at an uneven rate → pulp recession (pulp exposure during cavity preparation becomes less likely).
  • •Line of demarcation between primary and secondary dentin: the tubules change direction, continue in a wavier course, decrease in number.
  • •Fewer DTs per unit area because the pulpal wall surface area diminishes and the tubules sclerose more readily (reducing permeability and protecting the pulp).
🩹 Tertiary Dentin
  • •Other names: irregular secondary, reparative, reactionary dentin; occurs with a severe stimulus (severe abrasion, caries, cavity preparation); formed on the exact localized area corresponding to the pulpal end of the exposed tubules to seal off the zone.
  • •Reactive: injured odontoblasts continue to form irregular dentin. Reparative: odontoblasts degenerate and are replaced by undifferentiated pulp cells.
  • •Types: tubular (fewer, irregular tubules) · atubular (no tubules) · osteodentin (trapped cells) · vasodentin (trapped blood vessel).
🩺 Clinical Considerations & Smear Layer
  • •Sensitivity is not caries unless the pulp is inflamed · air-driven instruments damage odontoblasts · indirect pulp capping (Ca(OH)2) → reparative dentin · permeability: surface substances reach the pulp · color: thick dentin → yellowing, pulp insulation, harder vitality test · localized tertiary dentin → smaller chamber, harder endodontics, perforation risk · smear layer.
  • •Smear layer: debris from dentin instrumentation; entry depends on particle size and tubule diameter; ↓ permeability, but interferes with bonding and is not removed by irrigation (acid conditioning removes it but can damage the pulp).
⚡ Dentin Sensitivity & Theories
  • •Enamel and cementum are non-sensitive; dentin is sensitive but not uniformly: most sensitive at the ADJ, quite sensitive near the pulp.
  • •Direct neural stimulation (oldest): nerve endings in dentin. Odontoblastic transduction: the process is excited and the impulse passes from the ADJ to nerve endings at the cell body. Fluid (hydrodynamic) theory (most accepted): fluid movement in the tubules stimulates nerve endings in the plexus of Rashcow.
  • •Evidence: fluid blebs on exposed dentin; air or cotton drying causes more fluid loss and more pain.
نظرية الـ hydrodynamic هي الأكثر قبولاً: حركة السائل داخل الأنيبيبات تنبه الأعصاب.

Lecture 5 — Cementum Structure

Classification · properties · histology · CEJ · functions · age changes · hypercementosis

📘 Definition & Classification
  • •A thin layer of hard mineralized connective tissue covering the anatomical roots; resembles bone but is not vascular and has little turnover; anchors gingival and periodontal fibers.
  • •Classified by: development (primary / secondary) · cellularity (acellular / cellular) · origin of collagen fibers (extrinsic / intrinsic / mixed).
  • •Primary: formed during root formation, before its completion. Secondary: after completion of the root.
الملاط طبقة رقيقة تغطي الجذر، يشبه العظم لكنه غير وعائي وقليل التجدد، ووظيفته تثبيت ألياف الـ PDL.
🧩 Acellular vs Cellular Cementum
  • •Acellular: clear and structureless; covers the cervical two thirds (over the granular layer of Tomes); Sharpey’s fibers are inserted into it; formed of calcified ground substance + the embedded portion of Sharpey’s fibers.
  • •Cellular: same structure plus embedded cementocytes; found in the apical one third.
  • •When the cementum is thin, Sharpey’s fibers cross its entire thickness; with more deposition, a larger part of the fibers is incorporated.
  • •Thickness does not strengthen attachment: the deeper portion of the fibers is calcified and the attachment proper is confined to the superficial layer of cementum only.
🧵 Origin of the Collagenous Matrix
  • •Extrinsic fiber cementum: Sharpey’s fibers produced by the fibroblasts of the PDL.
  • •Intrinsic fiber cementum: fibers produced by the developed cementoblasts.
  • •Mixed fiber cementum: fibers from both fibroblasts and cementoblasts.
📏 Physical Properties & Composition
  • •Thickness: 20–50 µ at the cervical area → maximum 150–200 µ at the apex and bifurcation area.
  • •Color: pale yellow; distinguished from enamel by its lack of luster and darker hue. Permeability: from the dentin and PDL sides.
  • •Composition: 45–50% inorganic (calcium phosphate, hydroxyapatite) · 50–55% organic (collagen: 90% type I, 5% type III, 5% types VII, V and XIV, plus protein, polysaccharides, trace elements).
  • •Cementum contains the greatest amount of fluoride of all mineralized tissues.
🔬 Histology: Cementocytes & Lines of Salter
  • •Cementocytes: similar to osteocytes (granular cytoplasm, faintly stained nucleus); processes anastomose and are mostly directed toward the PDL for nutrition; the cell lies in a lacuna and its processes run in canaliculi; the deepest ones show signs of degeneration.
  • •Incremental lines of Salter: separate cementum into layers (periodic formation); highly mineralized, less collagen, more ground substance; roughly parallel to the long axis of the root; further apart in cellular cementum (faster formation).
🔗 Cemento-Enamel Junction (CEJ)
  • •Edge to edge (30%): proper development; the edges meet in a sharp line.
  • •Denuded dentin (10%): Hertwig’s epithelial root sheath delayed in separating from the dentin → a zone of root dentin without cementum; very sensitive, especially with gingival recession.
  • •Cementum overlaps enamel (60%): local disruption (premature separation) of the reduced dental epithelium → follicular cells contact the enamel and become cementoblasts; may appear as an island of afibrillar cementum.
⚙️ Functions & ⏳ Age Changes
  • •Functions: (1) medium for attachment of PDL collagen fibers to the alveolar bone (and maintains the attachment apparatus by continuous deposition); (2) compensates the tooth structure lost by wear or fracture by apical deposition; (3) repairs root areas damaged by resorption.
  • •Age changes (6): irregular surface (calcified ligament fiber bundles) · continuous apical deposition (a two-edged weapon: maintains tooth length but obstructs the apical foramen) · reversal lines · root dentin resorption covered by cemental repair · permeability decreases gradually · cementicles.
  • •Cementicles: calcified ovoid/rounded nodules in the PDL near the cemental surface; free, attached or embedded; appear with aging or at a site of trauma.
📈 Hypercementosis & 🩺 Clinical Considerations
  • •Hypercementosis: abnormal thickening of cementum. Hypertrophy: physiologic, in good functioning teeth (more root surface for PDL fibers). Hyperplasia: pathologic overgrowth in a non-functioning or embedded tooth; no Sharpey’s fibers.
  • •Cellular cementum has no nerves → no pain in scaling (but removal of cementum exposes dentin and causes sensitivity). Cementum is resistant to resorption (especially in younger patients) → orthodontic movement resorbs alveolar bone, not the root; its high fluoride content may add to this resistance.

Lecture 5 — Cementum Structure

Classification · properties · histology · CEJ · functions · age changes · hypercementosis

✍️ أسئلة مقالية متوقعة بإجابات مختصرة ووافية. اضغط «إخفاء الإجابات» لتجيب بنفسك أولاً ثم اكشف الإجابة.

❓ 1. Define cementum and classify it.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •A thin layer of hard mineralized connective tissue covering the anatomical roots; resembles bone but is not vascular and has little turnover; anchors gingival and periodontal fibers.
  • •Classified according to development (primary, secondary), cellularity (acellular, cellular) and the origin of collagen fibers (extrinsic, intrinsic, mixed).
❓ 2. Compare acellular and cellular cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Acellular: clear and structureless; cervical two thirds; calcified ground substance + embedded portion of Sharpey’s fibers.
  • •Cellular: same structure with embedded cementocytes; apical one third; formed faster, so its incremental lines are further apart.
❓ 3. Does an increase in the thickness of cementum enhance the strength of attachment of the individual fibers? Why?
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •No. The deeper portion of the fibers is calcified, and the attachment proper is confined to the superficial layer of cementum only.
❓ 4. Classify cementum according to the origin of the collagenous matrix.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Extrinsic: Sharpey’s fibers produced by the fibroblasts of the PDL.
  • •Intrinsic: produced by the developed cementoblasts.
  • •Mixed: produced by both fibroblasts and cementoblasts.
❓ 5. Write on the physical properties and chemical composition of cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Thickness 20–50 µ at the cervical area → 150–200 µ at the apex and bifurcation; pale yellow (lacks luster, darker hue than enamel); permeable from the dentin and PDL sides.
  • •45–50% inorganic (hydroxyapatite) · 50–55% organic (mainly collagen: 90% type I); the greatest amount of fluoride of all mineralized tissues.
❓ 6. Write on cementocytes.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Similar to osteocytes (granular cytoplasm, faintly stained nucleus); the cell lies in a lacuna and its processes in canaliculi.
  • •The processes anastomose and are mostly directed toward the PDL for nutrition; those in the deepest layer show signs of degeneration.
❓ 7. Write on the incremental lines of Salter.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Separate cellular and acellular cementum into layers: periodic formation.
  • •Highly mineralized, with less collagen and more ground substance; roughly parallel to the long axis of the root.
  • •Further apart in cellular cementum because it forms faster.
❓ 8. Write on the cemento-enamel junction.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •The relation between the enamel and cementum edges at the cervix; three forms:
  • •Edge to edge 30% (proper development) · denuded dentin 10% (Hertwig’s sheath delayed; bare sensitive dentin) · cementum overlaps enamel 60% (premature separation of the reduced dental epithelium; afibrillar cementum).
❓ 9. Mention the functions of cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Medium for attachment of PDL collagen fibers to the alveolar bone, maintaining the attachment apparatus by continuous deposition.
  • •Compensation of lost tooth structure (wear or fracture) by apical deposition.
  • •Repair of root areas damaged by resorption.
❓ 10. Write on the age changes of cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Irregular surface · continuous apical deposition (two-edged weapon: maintains tooth length, obstructs the apical foramen) · reversal lines · root dentin resorption covered by cemental repair · decreasing permeability · cementicles.
❓ 11. What are cementicles?
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Calcified ovoid or rounded nodules in the PDL near the cemental surface; free, attached or embedded.
  • •Appear with aging or at a site of trauma; contain a nidus of epithelial cells.
❓ 12. Write on hypercementosis.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Abnormal thickening of cementum (one tooth or generalized).
  • •Hypertrophy: physiologic, in good functioning teeth. Hyperplasia: pathologic overgrowth in a non-functioning or embedded tooth; no Sharpey’s fibers.
❓ 13. Write on the clinical considerations of cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Cellular cementum has no nerves, so it is non-sensitive to pain (removal exposes dentin and causes sensitivity).
  • •Resistant to resorption, especially in younger patients: orthodontic tooth movement resorbs alveolar bone, not the root; its high fluoride content may contribute.

Comparisons — Enamel · Dentin · Cementum

The three hard tissues side by side

✍️ أسئلة المقارنة بين المينا والعاج والملاط. اضغط «إخفاء الإجابات» لتجيب بنفسك أولاً ثم اكشف الإجابة.

❓ 1. Compare enamel, dentin and cementum regarding their chemical composition.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel: 96% inorganic (hydroxyapatite); 4% organic + water (amelogenins and enamelin).
  • •Dentin: 70% inorganic; 20% organic (collagen 90%); 10% water.
  • •Cementum: 45–50% inorganic; 50–55% organic (mainly collagen: 90% type I); the greatest amount of fluoride.
❓ 2. Compare enamel, dentin and cementum regarding cells and ability to repair.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel: acellular, non-vital; cannot regenerate because the ameloblasts are lost at eruption.
  • •Dentin: living, vital; repairs by odontoblast deposition (secondary and tertiary dentin).
  • •Cementum: acellular (cervical two thirds) and cellular (apical one third); not vascular; repairs damaged root areas and compensates for wear by apical deposition.
❓ 3. Compare the incremental lines of enamel, dentin and cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel: cross striations (daily, 3–4 µm), striae of Retzius (weekly) with perikymata on the surface, neonatal line.
  • •Dentin: von Ebner lines (daily, 4–8 micron in the crown), contour lines of Owen (accentuated), neonatal line.
  • •Cementum: lines of Salter (periodic formation) — hypercalcified, with less collagen and more ground substance.
❓ 4. Compare the hypocalcified structures of enamel and dentin.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel: lamellae, tufts and spindles.
  • •Dentin: inter-globular dentin (crown) and Tomes’ granular layer (root, next to the cementum).
❓ 5. Define the ADJ, the DCJ and the CEJ.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •ADJ (DEJ): junction between enamel and dentin; scalloped; the most sensitive area of dentin.
  • •DCJ: junction between dentin and cementum; usually smooth and masked by Tomes’ granular layer (scalloped in deciduous teeth).
  • •CEJ: relation between the cementum and enamel edges at the cervix: edge to edge 30%, denuded dentin 10%, overlap 60%.
❓ 6. Compare the age changes of enamel, dentin and cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel: attrition, color changes, surface modification (ionic exchange), decreased permeability.
  • •Dentin: darker color, sclerotic dentin, dead tracts, regular secondary dentin (pulp recession), tertiary dentin.
  • •Cementum: irregular surface, continuous apical deposition, reversal lines, root dentin resorption repaired, decreased permeability, cementicles.
❓ 7. Compare the permeability of enamel, dentin and cementum.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel: semi-permeable; mainly from saliva to the outer layer.
  • •Dentin: permeable through the tubules (profuse branching increases it); decreases with age.
  • •Cementum: permeable from the dentin and PDL sides; decreases gradually with age.
❓ 8. Compare enamel, dentin and cementum regarding sensitivity.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Enamel: insensitive. Cementum: non-sensitive (no nerves).
  • •Dentin: sensitive, but not uniformly: most at the ADJ and quite sensitive near the pulp (hydrodynamic theory).
❓ 9. Compare enamel, dentin and cementum regarding thickness and hardness.
👁️ اضغط لإظهار الإجابة
  • •Thickness: enamel 2–2.5 mm at the cusps (knife edge at the cervix); dentin 3–10 mm or more; cementum 20–50 µ at the cervix up to 150–200 µ at the apex.
  • •Hardness: enamel is the hardest; dentin is less hard than enamel but harder than cementum and bone.
